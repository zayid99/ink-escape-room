# MARGINALIA — Story Bible

> *"Don't trust the clocks. Don't trust me either. — ~~W~~ M"*
> — red ink, inside the front cover

This document is the single source of truth for the story of **Marginalia**, a 2D
psychological-horror escape-room game drawn as the pages of a journal. Everything
the player sees — rooms, objects, people — is ink on paper. Red pen is reserved
for fire, annotations and the clues that matter.

It contains spoilers for every chapter.

---

## 1. Premise (what the player is told)

Gannet Island is a tidal island off a grey northern coast. It is joined to the
mainland by a stone causeway that is only walkable for a few hours around low
water. On it stands **Wexley House**, the home of the Lark family: a clockmaker's
house full of stopped clocks.

The player is **Maren Lark**, thirty-five. Her father, Edmund, died in September.
Her younger sister **Wren** came over to the island to empty the house and put it
up for sale. Wren stopped answering her phone. Maren took the last ferry across.

She wakes at a desk in the attic, by candlelight, at **3:17 a.m.**, with her
journal open in front of her. The door is locked from the outside. Someone has
written in her journal in red pen.

**Stated goal:** find Wren.

---

## 2. The truth (what actually happened)

### 2004 — the fire

* Edmund Lark kept every clock in Wexley House **40 minutes fast** — "Lark time" —
  so that no one in the family would ever misjudge the tide on the causeway.
* In the winter of 2004 the old coal-converted boiler in the cellar cracked its
  heat exchanger. Whenever the heating ran, **carbon monoxide** seeped up through
  the house, worst in the rooms above the back stair.
* The family began to suffer: headaches, confusion, lost hours. The canaries in
  Clara's (the mother's) greenhouse died. Clara and both girls began to see
  **"the tall man"** — a figure in doorways that appeared "when the radiators
  knock". (Carbon-monoxide poisoning is well documented to cause a sensed or seen
  "presence".) The girls believed light kept him away. Wren, aged nine, began
  lighting a candle every night.
* Maren, thirteen, drew everything in a sketchbook. Wren had a habit of
  "correcting" her sister's drawings in **red pen** — the sisters called it
  "marking the margins".
* **14 February 2004.** The cracked flue ignited the timber around it at about
  **2:20 a.m. true time**. The fire climbed the back stair like a chimney. At
  **2:37 true time** the heat reached the landing and stopped the longcase clock.
  Because it ran on Lark time, its hands read **3:17**.
* Wren woke to smoke at the same moment her candle fell from the sill and the
  nursery curtain caught. She has believed for twenty-two years that **her candle
  started the fire**.
* Maren got Wren out of the nursery window onto the porch roof, where **Abel
  Rook** — the ferryman, who had seen the glow from the mainland and run the
  causeway — caught her. Maren went back in for their father, who lay unconscious
  in his workshop. Firemen found Edmund alive at the foot of the back stair, where
  Maren had dragged him. **Maren was found beside him. She died of smoke
  inhalation. She was thirteen.**
* Wren climbed out clutching the only thing she saved: **Maren's sketchbook.**
* The inquest found the fire began at the boiler flue and that "a candle in the
  nursery played no part in the ignition". Edmund never showed nine-year-old Wren
  the report. He locked it in his workshop, capped the boiler, stopped every
  clock in the house at the minute it happened, padlocked the nursery, and never
  spoke of that night again.
* Wren was sent to Aunt Hester in Leeds for a year (2005), then came back to live
  with her father until she was eighteen. Her mother, Clara, left the island in
  2005 and died in 2011.

### 2026 — the present

* Edmund dies in September. Wren, now thirty-one, inherits the house.
* **10 November.** Wren crosses to the island alone to clear it. It is bitterly
  cold. She relights the boiler that her father capped twenty-two years ago.
* The carbon monoxide returns. So do the headaches, the lost hours — and the
  tall man.
* Under the poison and the grief, Wren slides into an old coping mechanism: she
  writes in Maren's sketchbook **as Maren**, in black ink. She "becomes" the
  sister who survived in her head, and the sister she is looking for is herself.
  When her own voice breaks through, it comes out the way it always did: **in red
  pen, in the margins.**
* Every night she wakes at "3:17", remembers nothing of the previous days, reads
  her own red notes and takes them for someone else's.
* Abel Rook, now seventy, comes across at each low water to check on her. On the
  third night he found her walking knee-deep into the rising tide. Since then he
  locks her bedroom door and the stair door when he leaves, wedges the attic
  window open for air, and leaves notes. He has called a doctor, who comes on the
  morning boat. He doesn't know about the boiler being relit — he only knows the
  house is "doing to her what it did to her father".
* **The game begins on the fourth night.**

## 4. Chapters

Each chapter: introduces information → raises new questions → gives an
objective → introduces puzzles → reveals part of the mystery → adds uncertainty →
connects to earlier clues → builds to the final revelation.

### Chapter 1 — *The Margin* (Attic: Study, Landing, Nursery) — **playable**

* **New information:** Wren's things are here; someone called "A.R." locks this
  room at night and leaves notes addressed to Wren; Father's clocks run on "Lark
  time"; the nursery has been padlocked since the fire.
* **Questions:** Who is A.R.? Why is he locking Wren in? Who is writing in red in
  my journal — Wren? Why is the radiator warm when Father never used the heating?
* **Objective:** Get out of the attic and find Wren.
* **Puzzles:**
  1. **The Locked Door** — the key is in the lock on the far side. Slide the
     *tide table* under the door, push the key out with a *hairpin*, pull the
     paper back. (The tide table is kept: it matters in Chapter 4.)
  2. **The True Minute** — Father's combination padlock on the nursery reads
     *"Only the true minute opens this. — E."* The longcase clock is stopped at
     3:17, but Father's ledger reveals every clock runs 40 minutes fast.
     Code: **0237**. Entering 0317 fails and the journal answers in red: *"Told
     you. Don't trust the clocks."*
  3. **From Memory** — on entering the nursery, the journal shows a drawing of the
     room "from memory". Three details are wrong: the bed labels, the curtains,
     and the wall where the wardrobe stands (the dollhouse's miniature nursery
     shows a little door there). Correcting all three makes the page redraw
     itself, the wardrobe can be pushed aside, and the old back-stair hatch is
     revealed.
* **Reveal (Twist 1):** On the hatch, in red: four tally marks and *"Maren — check
  your coat pocket."* In the pocket is a red fineliner — the one missing from
  Wren's box — with its cap chewed flat. *Wren chews her pens. I don't.* The red
  notes were never Wren's. They are hers, from three nights she cannot remember.
* **Uncertainty:** If she has been here four nights, where has she been in the
  day? And if she is the one writing to herself as "Maren"… why did one note start
  with a W?

### Chapter 2 — *Lark Time* (Ground floor: Kitchen, Hall, Workshop) — **playable**

* **New information:** Somebody was in the kitchen tonight — a warm kettle, a
  stranger's tea (milk, two sugars), wet boot prints from the bolted back door.
  Wren's dead phone with a pencilled note: "Charged it for you. Ring me. — A."
  Father's calendar, still on March: "14 — W's birthday — 31!". The kitchen clock
  is the only clock still running (Lark time, in real time). Father's workshop:
  a wall of clocks at 3:17 and the regulator — the house's one honest clock.
* **Objective:** Find out who A.R. is.
* **Puzzles:**
  1. **The Tape** — the answerphone cassette has been snapped. Mend it with sticky
     tape from the dresser (item combination) and play it: Abel Rook on the phone
     to a doctor ("Edmund's girl… she keeps saying she has to find Wren"; "I saw
     the glow at twenty-five past two by my watch" in 2004; "I've hidden
     Edmund's keys in the bread crock") and a message to Wren ("the front-door
     code is your birthday"). The bread crock is only worth searching once the
     tape has been heard.
  2. **The True Clock** — the regulator has lost its pendulum (it is propping the
     hall cupboard open). Hang it, then set the regulator to the **true** time.
     The kitchen clock runs in real time on Lark time, so the answer changes as
     you play: read the kitchen clock and subtract forty minutes. Entering the
     kitchen clock's reading is answered in red: "Lark time. It wants the TRUTH."
     It strikes, and Father's cabinet (latched to the strike) opens: Mother's
     2004 diary and her keys.
  3. **The Mirror and the Window** — after the diary the radiators knock, and the
     tall man stands in the hall mirror whenever she turns her back on it. Catch
     him with the torch and he is gone. To understand him she must look *out of
     the window* while he is in the glass: Abel's lantern is far out on the
     causeway.
* **Reveal (Twist 2):** Two presences at once. **The tall man is not Abel. The
  house is poisoning her — carbon monoxide from the boiler — as it poisoned her
  family in 2004. And she relit it: "It's freezing — I got the boiler going,"
  Night 1, her handwriting.** Abel has been opening the windows.
* **Uncertainty:** Abel's notes are all to "Wren". Has he mistaken her for her
  sister — or has he never known there were two of them?

### Chapter 3 — *Exposure* (Glasshouse, Darkroom, Cellar) — **playable**

* **New information:** The chirp heard since Chapter 1 is a carbon-monoxide alarm
  Abel cable-tied to the boiler pipe in Clara's glasshouse ("A.R. — keep this
  ON"), its battery dying. Clara's canaries are still in their cage. Her
  darkroom holds an undeveloped roll: "FEB 04 — girls". The cellar is chained,
  chalked NEVER.
* **Objective:** Find the leak; shut the boiler down.
* **Puzzles:**
  1. **The Alarm** — the battery from the attic desk drawer (Chapter 1, wrapped in
     "SHUT UP SHUT UP SHUT UP") brings it back: 190 ppm. Carried, it shows a live
     reading that rises toward the source — a hot/cold search.
  2. **Exposure** — develop the film. Safelight on, **torch off** (white light
     ruins it), then pour four unlabelled-by-step bottles in Clara's order
     (developer in brown glass → stop "the vinegar one" → fix "hypo" → wash).
     Her light-tight paper safe, opened in the dark, holds the cellar key.
  3. **Shutting Her Down** — in the cellar (bad air: open the coal chute or be
     driven back upstairs), follow the alarm to the cracked flue joint, then shut
     the boiler down in the order on Father's brass tag: gas, check the pilot,
     pump, air. Wrong orders blow back or make the pipes bang.
* **Reveal (Twist 3):** The photographs: "M. (13) & W. (9) — Wren 'correcting' as
  usual." Behind the cold boiler, in a tobacco tin marked "Where it happened. Not
  for Wren. Not yet.": the inquest. **Maren Elizabeth Lark, aged 13, died in the
  fire.** It began at the boiler flue at 2:20 a.m.; the candle "played no part".
* **Uncertainty:** "Then who am I?"

### Chapter 4 — *Low Water* (Back Stair, Nursery, Hall, Causeway) — **playable**

* **New information:** Dawn; the house clocks say 6:00. The back stair, walked
  properly for the first time: burned black, Father's "M." and a cross on the
  bottom step, a clock fallen by the cellar door (3:02) and one on the
  half-landing (3:09). The journal's first page: "MAREN LARK — age 13" and, in a
  nine-year-old's red fist, "W. LARK, age 9 — you drew my bed wrong".
* **Objective:** Leave the island alive.
* **Puzzles:**
  1. **The Night of the Fire** — put five events in order by **true** time: flue
     catches (2:20, inquest) → cellar-door clock (3:02 Lark = 2:22) → Abel runs
     (2:25, his own watch, from the tape) → half-landing clock (3:09 = 2:29) →
     the curtain catches as the longcase stops (3:17 = 2:37). The candle fell
     last.
  2. **The Last Page** — sign the journal. *Wren* needs enough evidence (four of:
     red pen, headboard, height marks, calendar, photographs, inquest, fire
     route, Abel's note); *Maren* is always possible (loop ending).
  3. **Low Water** — the front door (Abel's padlock: her birthday, 1403). The
     choice offers "Set out now" or "Wait ten minutes", showing the **house**
     time. The tide table says 7:49, safe ±2 hours, in **true** time. At 6:00
     by the house it is really 5:20 — 29 minutes too early. Every window in the
     house shows the causeway emerging by true time, as an honest hint.
* **Final revelation:** see §6.

## 5. The three major twists

1. **The red notes are mine** (end of Chapter 1). The "helpful/hostile other" in
   the margins is the protagonist herself, writing on nights she can't remember.
   *Foreshadowed by:* the red pen missing from the box in the suitcase; the
   chewed cap; the note signed with a crossed-out W; the ferry ticket dated four
   days ago; the window that "keeps opening"; the wastebasket full of the same
   drawing; "3:17 again?" in red on the clock.
2. **The tall man is the house's breath — and A.R. is a protector** (end of
   Chapter 2). The haunting is carbon monoxide from the boiler; the frightening
   stranger is the man keeping her alive.
   *Foreshadowed by:* the radiator being warm in Chapter 1 ("Father never used the
   heating"); Maren's 2004 drawing note "he only comes when the radiators knock";
   Abel's "Keep the window open a crack. Don't relight anything."; the alarm with
   its battery removed; the low-battery chirp every forty seconds; the candle that
   burns low and lazy; the haze that thickens when the window is shut.
3. **Maren died in 2004** (end of Chapter 3).
   *Foreshadowed by:* the height marks — M's stop in 2004; the "family story"
   that she "went to Aunt Hester's" (the W marks skip 2005 — Wren is the one who
   went); the journal's early drawings in a 13-year-old's hand; "Maren"'s drawing
   of the nursery labels the window bed as hers, but the headboard is carved
   WREN.

## 6. The final revelation

**You are Wren.** The missing sister you crossed the water to find is you. You
have been living inside your dead sister's sketchbook, drawing the world as she
would have, and correcting it in red as you always did.

And the second half, the part that makes every earlier clue turn over:

**The fire was never your candle.** It began at the boiler at 2:20. The landing
clock stopped at 2:37 true time — **3:17 on Lark time** — the same minute your
candle fell. The "true minute" Father put on your door (Chapter 1, puzzle 2) was
never a password. It was the truth, locked on the door of the room you blamed
yourself for. Everything that has haunted you for twenty-two years — the tall
man, the hour 3:17, the fire, the guilt, the clocks — comes from **one machine**
in the cellar and **one lie the clocks told**. And four nights ago, frozen and
alone, you lit it again.

Connections the ending pulls together:

* the **padlock code 0237** (Ch. 1) = the inquest's ignition timeline (Ch. 3);
* the **tide table** (Ch. 1) = how you leave alive (Ch. 4), but only if you
  remember "Lark time" from the **ledger** (Ch. 1);
* the **red pen** (Ch. 1) = Wren's voice, who "corrected Maren's drawings in red"
  (journal pages, Ch. 1);
* the **scribbled face in the family photo** (Ch. 1) = Wren scratching herself out
  of the family she believed she destroyed;
* **"Don't trust me either. — ~~W~~ M"** (opening line) = exactly who wrote it;
* **Abel's notes "to Wren"** (Ch. 1–2) = notes to you;
* **"he only comes when the radiators knock"** (Ch. 1) = carbon monoxide (Ch. 2–3).

### Endings

* **"Wren" (true ending):** with enough evidence collected, the player can sign
  the final page in red: *Wren*. Abel is waiting on the causeway in grey dawn. He
  says her name and she answers to it. The journal redraws every room one last
  time — in daylight.
* **"Maren" (loop ending):** sign it *Maren* (or lack the evidence to sign
  anything else) and the last page turns to a fresh entry in black ink: *"Came
  over on the last ferry. Wren's not here. Her case is."* — Night 5.
* **Game over — "High Water":** leave by the house clocks instead of the tide
  table's true time and the causeway disappears under you.

### Red herrings (deliberate)

* **Abel Rook** — framed by his locks, his notes "to Wren", his lantern, the

* **"Wren's candle started the fire"** — the family myth. Disproved in Chapter 3.
* **"3:17" as a supernatural hour** — it's a clock running 40 minutes fast.
* **The music box that plays itself** (Ch 1) — never explained by the plot; see

## 8. Left unexplained (on purpose)

* The nursery music box plays four notes by itself the first time the player
  enters. The mechanism is wound down and rusted.
* In the true ending's final redrawn page, a thirteen-year-old girl is drawn at
  the nursery window, waving, in black ink. Wren didn't draw her.
* One red annotation in Chapter 1 ("Told you.") appears while the player is
  watching the padlock, with the pen still in the protagonist's pocket.

## 9. Style and voice rules

* The protagonist narrates in first person, short sentences, dry, frightened
  under the surface. She rationalises every contradiction quickly — the player
  should notice her doing it.
* Black ink = "Maren", the world as the protagonist insists it is.
  **Red pen = Wren**, fire, and anything true.
* Drawings change when belief changes: crossed out, circled, annotated or
  redrawn. Nothing changes without a reason the story can give.
* No jump scare without a story reason. Silence before discoveries.
